Bramble's devices sync with each other directly. Nothing in between keeps a copy of your vault, and
that is the point. It also means a change can only travel while two of your devices are unlocked
and online at the same time.

For a lot of people that happens on its own: a desktop app left running at home, a phone opened
during the day. For others it never quite does. The laptop is closed by the time the phone comes
out, the work computer is off all weekend, and a password changed on one is still the old one on
the other.

The mailbox closes that gap. Each device leaves its changes there, and the others collect them the
next time they are unlocked, whether or not anything else is online.

## A mailbox, not a cloud

With a mailbox, Bramble syncs as smoothly as a cloud password manager: change a password on your
phone, and your laptop has it the next time you open it. What it leaves out is the risk. A cloud
service keeps your whole vault on its servers for as long as you use it, where a breach or a court
order can reach it. We never keep a copy of your vault.

The mailbox holds changes in transit and nothing else. Each change is encrypted on your device
before it is sent, and the mailbox deletes it as soon as every one of your devices has collected
it. If one of them never comes back, its share goes after a year. Your vault itself stays on your
devices, the way it always has.

The mailbox cannot read what it holds. It knows your devices only by the keys they sign with, never
by name, and it never sees your master password or your vault key. What it does see is when your
devices check in and how much they store. Files you attach travel the same way, encrypted.

Direct sync carries on beside it. Devices that are online together still talk to each other
straight away; the mailbox is for the times they are not.

## Bramble's mailbox, or your own

Bramble runs a mailbox you can use for $2 a month, sold 6 to 36 months at a time. Each account
holds up to 1 GB at once, which is far more than sync normally needs, since everything is deleted
as soon as it is delivered.

Or run your own. The mailbox server is open source, under the AGPL, and runs on Node with
everything it stores in one folder. Point Bramble at it under **Settings → Sync → Mailbox →
Advanced**, and it costs nothing.

Sync between your devices stays free, as it always has. We made the mailbox as a way to support
Bramble: what it earns keeps the project going and pays for security audits. Charging for it also
keeps the cost of storing and serving other people's data off everyone who never uses it.

## An account with nothing to leak

A Bramble Mailbox account is a 25-digit number. There is no email address, no name and no
password. You create one on [bramble.sh/account](https://bramble.sh/account), add time, and enter
the number in Bramble.

Payments go through Stripe, and they are kept apart from the account on purpose. Stripe never sees
your account number. Bramble keeps a link between a payment and an account for 120 days, long
enough to handle a refund or a dispute, and then deletes it. Our own records of sales keep the
date, country, amount and tax, and no account.

Purchases can be refunded within 14 days, less the days since you bought them. You can delete the
account from the same page at any time.

The iPhone and iPad app doesn't sell time. Buy it on the website, then sign in with the number in
the app.

## When the time runs out

Nothing is lost. From 14 days before the end, Bramble says so on the vault list, with a way to buy
more or to stop using the mailbox. Once the time ends, your devices still collect whatever is
waiting for them. They just stop leaving new changes there, and go back to syncing when they are
online together.

## Getting started

The mailbox needs a vault that already syncs between two or more devices. Then open **Settings →
Sync**, and under **Mailbox** enter your account number, or your own server's address under
**Advanced**. Your other devices pick it up on their own.
